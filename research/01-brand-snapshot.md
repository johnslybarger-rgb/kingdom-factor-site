## Strategic brand architecture

**Recommendation:** build one Kingdom Factor website first, with two clearly separated lanes for John and Jim.

Why this matters:

- Preserves one SEO authority base.
- Keeps analytics, CMS, funnel tracking, and maintenance unified.
- Positions Kingdom Factor as the umbrella authority platform.
- Lets John and Jim serve distinct audiences without splitting the brand prematurely.
- Leaves room for future personal-brand microsites if the offers diverge.

- Leaders carrying meaningful responsibility for people, revenue, strategy, and stewardship.

### Jim / peer community and values lane

Source-backed audience traits:

## Offer and content pillars

1. **Christian Peer Advisory Groups** — trusted Christian leaders, accountability, prayerful discernment, practical business counsel, and shared learning.
2. **Executive Coaching Intensive** — 12-week private advisory for Christian business owners with weekly 1:1 virtual sessions, between-session support, and peer advisory integration.
3. **Leadership Circle** — free webinar / briefing introducing Christian leaders to a Christ-centered peer advisory model.
4. **Marketplace Ministry** — free webinar / content pathway for owners navigating profit, purpose, pressure, culture, ethics, and Kingdom impact.
5. **Kingdom Factor Collective** — story archive / interview initiative gathering examples of Christian business leaders integrating faith and excellence in the marketplace.
6. **Books and resources** — John and Jim author libraries as authority assets, source material, and possible lead magnets.
7. **Calculators / readiness assessments** — available in source inventory as inspiration, public use approval needed.

## Tone and voice

Recommended website voice:

- Wise, grounded, relational, and practical.
- Faith-integrated without sounding generic, preachy, or church-marketing clichéd.
- Premium advisory, but warm rather than corporate cold.
- Clear enough for busy owners who are carrying real responsibility.
- Serious about Scripture, stewardship, and marketplace pressure without overclaiming outcomes.

Avoid:

- Generic coaching language.
- Artificial hype.
- Unsupported transformation claims.
- Fake testimonials, fake stats, or invented member stories.
- Using “Christian” as decoration without practical business specificity.

## Color / typography direction

Initial direction from intake:

- Deep red / burgundy, black, cream, and warm neutral base.
- Use gold or muted brass sparingly for premium accent if it supports the logo.
- Avoid high-saturation “church conference” colors.
- Fonts should feel editorial, credible, and warm. Recommended pairing: refined serif for display headlines plus highly readable sans for body/UI.

Final palette and font pairings should be validated against actual logo assets before build.

## Proof policy

Do not invent or publish:

- Testimonials.
- Reviews.
- Member stories.
- Case studies.
- Client logos.
- Conversion rates.
- Revenue, member, or outcome stats.

Use these placeholders instead:

- `[REAL PROOF NEEDED]`
- `[CLIENT APPROVAL NEEDED]`
- `[SOURCE VERIFICATION NEEDED]`

Approval-gated source claims:

- Market-size claims from the marketing strategy document.
- Research/statistical claims in the Christian Business Peer Groups Executive Summary.
- Pricing and tier recommendations from the Executive Coaching Intensive document.
- Founding/history claims: 1998, 2022, 2025 partnership, “hundreds of members,” “thousands of groups facilitated.”
- Public use of book excerpts, calculators, and webinar scripts.
