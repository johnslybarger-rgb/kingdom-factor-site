# Kingdom Factor Design Direction

register: brand

## Visual north star

Premium Christian executive advisory: warm, composed, credible, confidential, and conversion-oriented. The site should feel like a private boardroom for serious Christian business owners, not a scrapbook, not a church flyer, and not an AI-generated landing page stack.

## Brand feel

- Warm cream, deep burgundy, dark charcoal, brass/gold accents.
- Large editorial serif is acceptable, but never so large that CTAs drop out of mobile view.
- Use cinematic advisory-room imagery as atmosphere, not as an oversized decorative object.
- Above-the-fold should immediately answer: who this is for, what pain it solves, and what to do next.

## Layout rules

- Desktop hero should be a strong two-column or layered editorial composition with visible CTAs.
- Mobile hero should prioritize headline, subhead, CTAs, and trust cues before large imagery.
- H1 must be customer-profile specific and outcome-oriented.
- Repeated card grids need hierarchy, varied rhythm, and stronger section framing.
- Reduce excessive vertical whitespace on mobile.
- Ensure cards feel premium: tighter copy, stronger borders, softer shadows, brass accents used sparingly.
- Navigation must be usable on desktop and mobile; desktop should not be hamburger-only unless intentionally premium and clearly tested.

## Copy rules

- Lead with Christian CEOs, owners, executives, and senior leaders.
- Use concrete pains: hard decisions, isolation, blind spots, growth pressure, accountability.
- Use concrete outcomes: wise counsel, sharper decisions, faithful stewardship, qualified peers, private coaching.
- Avoid vague hero lines like “a trusted room” unless followed by specific owner/executive language.
- Unapproved articles/resources remain draft or review-ready, not falsely published.

## Competitor lessons

- C12 is clear: do not lead alone; build a great business for a greater purpose; peer advisory is structured and transformative.
- Convene is clear: business performance plus eternal impact; stop leading alone; peer teams, coaching, and outcomes.
- Kingdom Factor should borrow the clarity of isolation/outcome language while keeping its own warmer, more intimate advisory-room tone.
